2025

  1. JHEP
    Accelerating template generation in resonant anomaly detection searches with optimal transport
    Matthew Leigh, Debajyoti Sengupta, Benjamin Nachman, and 1 more author
    Journal of High Energy Physics, 2025
    Abs DOI arXiv

    Introduces RAD-OT, which builds resonance-search background templates by solving an optimal transport problem between two side-band regions, achieving sensitivity comparable to deep-learning-based approaches with improved stability.

  2. EPJC
    Robust resonant anomaly detection with NPLM
    Gaia Grosso, Debajyoti Sengupta, Tobias Golling, and 1 more author
    European Physical Journal C, 2025
    Abs DOI arXiv

    Evaluates the New Physics Learning Machine (NPLM) as an alternative to CWoLa/BDT-based resonant anomaly detection, finding it outperforms BDT approaches in low-signal-injection scenarios while reducing sensitivity to hyperparameter choices.

2024

  1. SciPost
    CURTAINs Flows for Flows: Constructing Unobserved Regions with Maximum Likelihood Estimation
    Debajyoti Sengupta, Samuel Klein, John Andrew Raine, and 1 more author
    SciPost Physics, 2024
    Abs DOI arXiv

    An improved version of CURTAINs that trains the conditional normalizing flow between two side-band regions with maximum likelihood estimation instead of an optimal-transport loss, improving robustness and training speed while significantly increasing sensitivity over the original method.

  2. Phys. Rev. D
    PC-Droid: Faster Diffusion and Improved Quality for Particle Cloud Generation
    Matthew Leigh, Debajyoti Sengupta, John Andrew Raine, and 2 more authors
    Physical Review D, 2024
    Abs DOI arXiv Code

    Builds on PC-JeDi with a new diffusion formulation and updated integration solvers, trained on all jet types simultaneously to reach state-of-the-art particle-cloud generation quality with generation times up to two orders of magnitude faster than PC-JeDi.

  3. SciPost
    PC-JeDi: Diffusion for Particle Cloud Generation in High Energy Physics
    Matthew Leigh, Debajyoti Sengupta, Guillaume Quétant, and 3 more authors
    SciPost Physics, 2024
    Abs DOI arXiv

    Combines score-based diffusion models with transformers to generate LHC jets as particle clouds, reaching competitive fidelity against state-of-the-art generators with conditional control over jet mass and transverse momentum.

  4. JHEP
    Improving New Physics Searches with Diffusion Models for Event Observables and Jet Constituents
    Debajyoti Sengupta, Matthew Leigh, John Andrew Raine, and 2 more authors
    Journal of High Energy Physics, 2024
    Abs DOI arXiv

    Introduces Drapes, a diffusion-based background-template method for resonance searches that generates templates directly from noise or by partially diffusing existing data, reaching state-of-the-art performance on the LHCO di-jet dataset with both high-level and jet-constituent inputs.

  5. EPJC
    The Interplay of Machine Learning-Based Resonant Anomaly Detection Methods
    Tobias Golling, Gregor Kasieczka, Claudius Krause, and 6 more authors
    European Physical Journal C, 2024
    Abs DOI arXiv

    A comparative study of resonant anomaly detection methods on the LHC Olympics dataset, showing that different approaches flag different events as signal-like and that combining multiple methods can reduce false positives and improve sensitivity.

  6. Phys. Rev. D
    PIPPIN: Generating Variable Length Full Events from Partons
    Guillaume Quétant, John Andrew Raine, Matthew Leigh, and 2 more authors
    Physical Review D, 2024
    Abs DOI arXiv

    Combines transformers, score-based models, and normalizing flows to generate full detector-level events directly from parton-level information, handling the variable multiplicity between parton and reconstructed object spaces.

  7. MNRAS
    SkyCURTAINs: Model-Agnostic Search for Stellar Streams with Gaia Data
    Debajyoti Sengupta, Stephen Mulligan, David Shih, and 2 more authors
    Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society, 2024
    Abs DOI arXiv

    Adapts the CURTAINs method to search for stellar streams in Gaia data, building a background template from the correlation between a star’s features and its proper motion. Recovers the GD-1 stream with 75.4% purity, a 10-point improvement over prior ML-based methods, at 37.9% signal efficiency.

  8. EPJ Web Conf.
    Faster Simulated Track Reconstruction in the ATLAS Fast Chain
    William Leight, Martina Javurkova, Fang-Ying Tsai, and 3 more authors
    In EPJ Web of Conferences, 2024
    Abs DOI

    Presents speed improvements to simulated track reconstruction within the ATLAS Fast Chain, part of the ATLAS Collaboration’s fast-simulation program, reported on behalf of the ATLAS Computing Activity.

2023

  1. Front. Big Data
    CURTAINs for Your Sliding Window: Constructing Unobserved Regions by Transforming Adjacent Intervals
    John Andrew Raine, Samuel Klein, Debajyoti Sengupta, and 1 more author
    Frontiers in Big Data, 2023
    Abs DOI arXiv

    Introduces CURTAINs, which uses invertible neural networks to map side-band data into the signal region and build a model-independent background template for resonance searches. Matches leading bump-hunt approaches while training on a much narrower mass range in a fully data-driven way.

  2. arXiv
    EPiC-ly Fast Particle Cloud Generation with Flow-Matching and Diffusion
    Erik Buhmann, Cedric Ewen, Darius A. Faroughy, and 7 more authors
    arXiv preprint, 2023
    Abs arXiv

    Introduces EPiC-JeDi and EPiC-FM, an equivariant point-cloud diffusion model and a flow-matching model for LHC jet generation, both reaching state-of-the-art fidelity on the JetNet benchmarks with faster generation than transformer-based diffusion models.

  3. arXiv
    The Mass-ive Issue: Anomaly Detection in Jet Physics
    Tobias Golling, Takuya Nobe, Dimitrios Proios, and 8 more authors
    arXiv preprint, 2023
    Abs arXiv

    Highlights a core challenge in jet anomaly detection: preserving an unbiased estimate of jet mass. Using variational autoencoders and standard anomaly-detection metrics, shows that mass-sculpting is largely unavoidable across common approaches.
